How to make a PDF small enough to email

Short answer: keep the PDF under about 18 MB for Gmail or Outlook.com. Both say 25 MB, but email encoding makes attachments about a third larger on the way, so a 24 MB file usually bounces.

Email attachment limits

ServiceStated limitSafe file size
Gmail (personal)25 MBAbout 18 MB
Outlook.com25 MBAbout 18 MB
Work or school emailSet by your IT team, often 10 to 25 MBAsk, or aim for 7 to 10 MB

The limit counts all attachments in one email together, and the person receiving it can have a smaller limit than yours.

Why PDFs get so big

  • Scanned pages. Each page is a full photo, often at 300 to 600 dpi.
  • Photos inside the PDF straight from a phone camera, at full size.
  • Exported drawings with every layer and embedded image kept.

Text itself is tiny. Shrinking the pictures inside a PDF is what brings the size down, and it doesn't need to touch the text.

Ways to shrink it

  1. On iPhone or iPad: in FoldSize, add the PDF, tap the Gmail or Outlook limit, then tap Fit. Only the images inside are shrunk, so text stays sharp and searchable, and links and form fields keep working.
  2. On a Mac: open the PDF in Preview, choose File > Export, and set Quartz Filter to Reduce File Size. This can blur text on scanned pages, so check the result.
  3. On Windows: in Adobe Acrobat, use Reduce File Size or Optimize PDF.
  4. Split it. If it still won't fit, send two emails, or split the PDF into parts that each fit.
  5. Send a link instead. Gmail offers Google Drive and Outlook offers OneDrive for files over the limit, if the person receiving it can open them.

Before you send

  • Zoom in on the smallest text to make sure it's still readable.
  • If the PDF is digitally signed, shrink it first and sign it after. Any change to a signed PDF breaks the signature.
  • Be careful with free upload websites for private documents. They upload your file to someone else's server.
